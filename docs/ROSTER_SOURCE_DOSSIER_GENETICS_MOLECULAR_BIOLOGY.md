# Genetics and molecular biology roster source dossier

## Scope and method

This dossier supplies source material for the seven proposed fighters in the Genetics and
Molecular Biology category. Prize facts come from NobelPrize.org. Photo source pages were checked
on 2026-09-24; two reference images per fighter were downloaded only to
`/private/tmp/nobel-combat-m4-genetics/` for visual review. Character proportions, costumes, and
combat outcomes remain fictional game design.

## Jennifer Doudna

- **Nobel record:** Doudna shared the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, "for the development of a
  method for genome editing." [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2020/doudna/facts/)
- **Scientific hook:** The CRISPR-Cas9 system can be programmed with guide RNA for targeted DNA
  cleavage. [Jinek et al., _Science_ (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225829)
- **Photo references:** [UC Berkeley office portrait on a 2018 page](https://news.berkeley.edu/2018/01/18/berkeley-talks-transcript-jennifer-doudna-siddhartha-mukherjee/)
  and [2020 University of California Nobel story](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/news/jennifer-doudna-wins-2020-nobel-prize-chemistry)
  provide dated active-career references.
- **image_evaluator evidence:** Choose the UC Berkeley office reference: shoulder-length pale blonde-gray hair with a side part and
  inward-curled ends, small drop earrings, and a black zip-front top or jacket. The 2020 image
  corroborates light hair and jacket styling. Build, lower-body clothing, and footwear are not visible.
- **Guide RNA:** Real work: guide RNA directs Cas9 to a selected DNA sequence in the programmable
  system. [Jinek et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225829) Fictional caption:
  "Target acquired: one extremely rude chromosome."
- **Cas9 Cleavage:** Real work: Cas9 makes RNA-guided, site-specific DNA cuts.
  [Jinek et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225829) Fictional caption: "Molecular
  scissors, now with arena-sized handles."
- **CRISPR Rewrite:** Real work: CRISPR-Cas9 enabled a genome-editing method.
  [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2020/doudna/facts/) Fictional
  caption: "Patch notes: opponent now has fewer good ideas."

## Barbara McClintock

- **Nobel record:** McClintock received the 1983 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, "for her
  discovery of mobile genetic elements." [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1983/mcclintock/facts/)
- **Scientific hook:** Her maize work established that genetic elements can change position and
  alter nearby gene activity. [McClintock, _PNAS_ (1950)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.36.6.344)
- **Photo references:** [1947 Smithsonian laboratory photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Barbara_McClintock_%281902-1992%29_shown_in_her_laboratory_in_1947.jpg)
  and [1983 Nobel archive portrait](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1983/mcclintock/facts/)
  distinguish active maize research from her prize period.
- **image_evaluator evidence:** The retrieved 1947 reference is the modeling authority: dark short-to-medium
  hair swept back with a side wave, small round wire-frame glasses, a light buttoned lab smock with
  a wide pointed collar and round throat clasp, and a seated forward-leaning laboratory posture.
  Her right hand holds a slender tool beside a microscope. It establishes no standing figure,
  footwear, or rear silhouette. The 1983 portrait is a distinct period: gray side-swept hair,
  round glasses, and a knit sweater over a pointed collar. Use 1947; both sources support only the
  swept-away hair and small round glasses across periods.
- **Ac/Ds:** Real work: Ac and Ds are McClintock's mobile genetic elements in maize.
  [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1983/mcclintock/facts/) Fictional
  caption: "Two tiny troublemakers changed seats again."
- **Maize Chromosome:** Real work: McClintock used maize chromosomes to study heritable traits.
  [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1983/mcclintock/facts/) Fictional
  caption: "Corn has entered the chat, chromosomally."
- **TRANSPOSON:** Real work: mobile elements can change position on a chromosome.
  [McClintock (1950)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.36.6.344) Fictional caption: "Position changed.
  Please do not ask where the old position went."

## Elizabeth Blackburn

- **Nobel record:** Blackburn shared the 2009 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, "for the
  discovery of how chromosomes are protected by telomeres and the enzyme telomerase."
  [Nobel Prize press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2009/press-release/)
- **Scientific hook:** Telomerase synthesizes chromosome ends from an intrinsic RNA template.
  [Nobel advanced information](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2009/advanced-information/)
- **Photo references:** [2009 UPI Nobel-era headshot](https://www.upi.com/Science_News/2009/10/05/US-scientists-win-Nobel-Prize-medicine/14061254741564/)
  and [2011 National Portrait Gallery portrait](https://portrait.gov.au/portraits/2011.58/elizabeth-blackburn)
  cover recognition and continuing research leadership.
- **image_evaluator evidence:** Choose 2009. The portrait shows a loose gray-blonde bob, thin oval metal glasses, pale
  cream blazer, black top, and delicate pendant. The 2011 portrait corroborates the glasses and
  wavy bob while adding a full-torso, arms-folded pose; neither establishes lower-body clothing.
- **Telomere Cap:** Real work: telomeres protect chromosome ends from degradation.
  [Nobel Prize press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2009/press-release/)
  Fictional caption: "End caps installed. Warranty refuses to cover punches."
- **Telomerase:** Real work: telomerase synthesizes chromosome-end DNA.
  [Nobel advanced information](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2009/advanced-information/)
  Fictional caption: "One tiny, entirely game-only refill."
- **Replicative Senescence:** Real work: chromosome ends shorten across repeated cell divisions.
  [Nobel Prize press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2009/press-release/)
  Fictional caption: "You have divided too many times this round."

## Katalin Karik&oacute;

- **Nobel record:** Karik&oacute; shared the 2023 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, "for their
  discoveries concerning nucleoside base modifications that enabled the development of effective
  mRNA vaccines against COVID-19." [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2023/kariko/facts/)
- **Scientific hook:** Modified nucleosides reduced innate immune activation and increased protein
  production from in-vitro-transcribed mRNA. [Kariko et al., _Immunity_ (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2005.06.008)
- **Photo references:** [2021 Szeged portrait on Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Karik%C3%B3_Katalin_Szegeden.jpg)
  and [2023 Nobel portrait](https://www.nobelprize.org/all-nobel-prizes-2023/) provide dated late
  active-career and Nobel-era references.
- **image_evaluator evidence:** Choose 2021 for the full-character model: short side-swept brown hair, large
  angular glasses, black blazer over a burgundy top, dark straight-leg trousers, hands in pockets,
  and an upright stance. The 2023 close portrait corroborates hair and glasses but has a burgundy
  jacket. Build beneath clothing and footwear are not visible.
- **Modified Nucleoside:** Real work: nucleoside modifications reduced unwanted immune activation.
  [Kariko et al. (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2005.06.008) Fictional caption:
  "One letter swapped; drama reduced."
- **Lipid Nanoparticle:** Real work: lipid nanoparticles deliver mRNA in effective vaccines.
  [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2023/kariko/facts/) Fictional
  caption: "The bubble mail has arrived."
- **mRNA Translation:** Real work: mRNA transfers information that stimulates protein production.
  [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2023/kariko/facts/) Fictional
  caption: "Ribosome, please assemble a ridiculous amount of confetti."

## Sidney Altman

- **Nobel record:** Altman shared the 1989 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, "for their discovery of
  catalytic properties of RNA." [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1989/altman/facts/)
- **Scientific hook:** RNase P RNA alone can catalyze precursor-tRNA processing.
  [Guerrier-Takada et al., _Cell_ (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674%2883%2990535-8)
- **Photo references:** [1989 Nobel archive portrait](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1989/altman/facts/)
  and [2022 Yale News portrait](https://news.yale.edu/2022/04/06/sidney-altman-pathbreaking-scientist)
  provide prize-era and late-career references.
- **image_evaluator evidence:** Choose 1989: dark thick hair swept back, high forehead, clean-shaven face, no
  visible glasses, dark suit, light collared shirt, and patterned tie. The 2022 Yale image preserves
  swept hair, high forehead, clean-shaven face, and no visible glasses, while changing to a teal
  polo. Both are chest-up; build, hands, lower clothing, and footwear are not visible.
- **RNase P:** Real work: RNase P RNA can catalyze precursor-tRNA processing.
  [Guerrier-Takada et al. (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674%2883%2990535-8) Fictional
  caption: "Protein called in sick; RNA covered the shift."
- **RNA Catalyst:** Real work: Altman showed that RNA can catalyze a reaction.
  [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1989/altman/facts/) Fictional
  caption: "The arena has far too many self-starters."
- **RNA Is the Enzyme:** Real work: the 1989 prize recognized catalytic properties of RNA.
  [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1989/altman/facts/) Fictional
  caption: "Surprise: the enzyme brought no protein."

## Thomas Cech

- **Nobel record:** Cech shared the 1989 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, "for their discovery of
  catalytic properties of RNA." [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1989/cech/facts/)
- **Scientific hook:** His group found self-splicing RNA in _Tetrahymena_ rRNA precursor.
  [Kruger et al., _Cell_ (1982)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674%2882%2990425-6)
- **Photo references:** [2014 CU Boulder feature](https://www.colorado.edu/today/2014/01/15/cech-named-first-ever-national-commission-forensic-science)
  and [2019 CU Boulder feature](https://www.colorado.edu/today/2019/10/08/faculty-focus-tom-cech-looks-back-nobel-prize-chemistry)
  provide dated later-career references.
- **image_evaluator evidence:** Choose 2014: short gray hair with a receding hairline, rectangular dark glasses,
  pale blue button-down, folded arms, and upright torso. The 2019 reference corroborates hair,
  glasses, shirt, folded arms, and mountain backdrop. Both leave lower body and footwear not visible.
- **Group I Intron:** Real work: Cech studied a self-splicing group I intron.
  [Kruger et al. (1982)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674%2882%2990425-6) Fictional caption:
  "This loop has a very personal boundary issue."
- **Self-Splice:** Real work: the _Tetrahymena_ RNA precursor self-splices.
  [Kruger et al. (1982)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674%2882%2990425-6) Fictional caption:
  "Excuse me while I remove myself from this situation."
- **Ribozyme:** Real work: folded RNA can catalyze biochemical reactions.
  [Cech Nobel article](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1989/cech/article/) Fictional
  caption: "Folded, catalytic, and extremely dramatic."

## David Baltimore

- **Nobel record:** Baltimore shared the 1975 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, ``for their
  discoveries concerning the interaction between tumour viruses and the genetic material of the
  cell.'' [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1975/baltimore/facts/)
- **Scientific hook:** Reverse transcriptase synthesizes DNA from an RNA template.
  [Baltimore, _Nature_ (1970)](https://doi.org/10.1038/2261209a0)
- **Photo references:** [1975 Nobel Foundation archive portrait](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1975/baltimore/facts/)
  provides the prize-era image. The [Nobel interview transcript, 26 April 2001](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1975/baltimore/interview/)
  supplies later-career interview provenance; it is not the source of the portrait.
- **image_evaluator evidence:** Choose 1975. The portrait shows dark hair swept back with pronounced sideburns, large
  rectangular metal-frame glasses, a full dark beard and mustache, a dark jacket, light shirt, and
  patterned tie. It is an upper-torso source; use its 1970s styling rather than a present-day look.
- **Reverse Transcriptase:** Real work: reverse transcriptase makes a DNA copy from RNA.
  [Baltimore (1970)](https://doi.org/10.1038/2261209a0) Fictional caption: "The arrow has changed
  its mind and its alphabet."
- **Proviral Integration:** Real work: the prize concerned tumour-virus interactions with cellular
  genetic material. [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1975/baltimore/facts/)
  Fictional caption: "A blinking marker has filed the world's least welcome paperwork."
- **Central Dogma Reversed:** Real work: Baltimore's reverse-transcription discovery changed how
  researchers understood information flow. [Nobel Prize press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1975/press-release/)
  Fictional caption: "Arrows are now pointing the wrong way on purpose."

## Evidence limits

- Prize citations and research links establish awards and scientific hooks. They do not prescribe
  clothing, apparent age, height, or game balance.
- Photo choices are reference material for a stylized, non-photorealistic character. The requested
  game names and special concepts remain the authority for roster intent.
- Every caption above explicitly marks its combat outcome as fiction. None describes clinical care,
  vaccination advice, genome editing in people, or an actual biological effect on a combatant.
