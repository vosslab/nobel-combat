# Otto Heinrich Warburg source dossier

## Scope

This dossier supplies bounded source material for the first Nobel fighter. It supports visual and
research motifs without making biomedical claims about cancer treatment.

## Primary-source facts

- Otto Heinrich Warburg received the 1931 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for the discovery
  of the nature and mode of action of the respiratory enzyme. [Nobel Prize 1931 summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1931/summary/)
- In his 1931 Nobel lecture, Warburg described cellular respiration as catalytic oxidation involving
  a respiratory oxygen-transferring iron compound. [Warburg, "The Oxygen-Transferring Ferment of
  Respiration" (1931)](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/warburg-lecture.pdf)
- In 1956, Warburg reported respiration and lactic-acid fermentation measurements for cancer cells,
  including measurements at oxygen saturation. [Warburg, _Science_ 123 (1956), 309-314](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.123.3191.309)

## Visual identity

Warburg has a dark charcoal laboratory coat with iron-red piping, a small round pressure gauge at
the belt, and a protected glass manometer tube on the forearm. These are research identifiers, not
injury effects. [Warburg lecture (1931)](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/warburg-lecture.pdf)

## Research-motif specials

Warburg's three Special releases are fictional game effects selected by the shared meter. They do
not model tumor biology, imply that lactate powers movement, or claim a therapeutic effect.

- **Lactate Drive.** Real work: Warburg measured lactic-acid fermentation in cancer cells.
  [Warburg (1956)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.123.3191.309). Game fiction: a reposition dash
  followed by a strike.
- **Aerobic Glycolysis.** Real work: the same paper reports respiration and fermentation at oxygen
  saturation. [Warburg (1956)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.123.3191.309). Game fiction: a
  temporary self-speed modifier.
- **Oxygen Transfer Surge.** Real work: Warburg described a respiratory oxygen-transferring iron
  compound. [Warburg lecture (1931)](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/warburg-lecture.pdf).
  Game fiction: a strike followed by a short ring zone.

## Evidence limits

The sources establish respiration and fermentation research. They do not establish combat
techniques, damage, timing, or visual effects; those remain authored game data.
