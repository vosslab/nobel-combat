# Cell biology and neuroscience roster sources

This dossier supplies bounded research and likeness evidence for the two proposed fighters in
the Cell Biology and Neuroscience roster category. It supports an exaggerated game fiction;
it does not make biomedical, diagnostic, or therapeutic claims.

## Rita Levi-Montalcini

### Prize record

Rita Levi-Montalcini received the 1986 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, shared with
Stanley Cohen, "for their discoveries of growth factors." The chooser source is her
[Nobel Prize facts page](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1986/levi-montalcini/facts/).
That page says that in 1952 she isolated a substance from mouse tumors that caused vigorous
nervous-system growth in chicken embryos.

### Research-motif powers

#### NGF

**Real hook.** Nerve growth factor is the named research subject in Levi-Montalcini's Nobel
[lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/levi-montalcini-lecture.pdf), and the Nobel
facts page documents its growth-promoting effect in the original chick-embryo experiments.

**Game fiction.** Neurites burst from the floor and pursue the opponent. This is a symbolic,
impossible arena effect, not a simulation of neuronal growth.

**Caption.** "The nerves have decided to branch out."

#### Axon guidance

**Real hook.** Her Nobel [biographical record](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1986/levi-montalcini/biographical/)
describes her embryological work after Viktor Hamburger's chick-embryo research; her Nobel lecture
describes NGF's effects on sensory and sympathetic neuronal populations. Those are the scientific
roots for a growing-neurite visual, while target tracking is game fiction.

**Game fiction.** Growing axons navigate around props and curve toward the opponent with
impossible precision.

**Caption.** "Every wrong turn becomes a dendrite."

#### Growth factor

**Real hook.** The 1986 prize motivation is explicitly discoveries of growth factors, and the
[Nobel press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1986/press-release/1000/)
describes the tumor-and-chick-embryo observations that led to the factor.

**Game fiction.** A stage-wide neuron network branches until it tangles the opponent. It does not
represent a biological treatment or a real physiological outcome.

**Caption.** "One tiny signal; suddenly the arena needs pruning."

## Linda B. Buck

### Prize record

Linda B. Buck received the 2004 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, shared with Richard Axel,
"for their discoveries of odorant receptors and the organization of the olfactory system." The
chooser source is her [Nobel Prize facts page](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2004/buck/facts/).
It explains that odorants activate receptor-dependent signals that reach the brain.

### Research-motif powers

#### Odorant receptor

**Real hook.** Buck and Axel's 1991 [primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1840504/)
reported a large multigene family likely to encode odorant receptors in olfactory epithelium.

**Game fiction.** Buck selects a glowing receptor combination that tracks an opponent through an
opaque prop. It is a comic targeting device, not a claim that odor receptors provide vision.

**Caption.** "That smell has a name, an address, and nowhere to hide."

#### Combinatorial code

**Real hook.** The 1999 primary study [Combinatorial Receptor Codes for
Odors](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674(00)80581-4) found that one receptor can recognize
multiple odorants and one odorant can be recognized by multiple receptors, forming a
combinatorial code.

**Game fiction.** Individually weak scent puffs combine into a broad arena attack. No real odor
is assigned to the move.

**Caption.** "Three notes make a chord. This chord makes everyone leave."

#### OLFACTORY OVERLOAD

**Real hook.** Buck's [Nobel Prize facts page](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2004/buck/facts/)
explains that differing odorant sensors and their signals let the brain interpret scents; the prize
also covers organization of the olfactory system.

**Game fiction.** Every receptor fires at once as an absurd visible cloud, causing a comic
collapse. It is not a medical description of sensory overload.

**Caption.** "The nose filed a group complaint."
