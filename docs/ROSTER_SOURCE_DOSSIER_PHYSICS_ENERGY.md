# Physics and energy roster sources

This dossier supplies bounded research and likeness evidence for four proposed fighters in the
Physics and Energy roster category. It supports exaggerated game fiction; it does not make
scientific, engineering, or safety claims.

## John B. Goodenough

### Prize record

John B. Goodenough received the 2019 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, shared with M. Stanley Whittingham
and Akira Yoshino, "for the development of lithium-ion batteries." His [Nobel Prize facts
page](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2019/goodenough/facts/) says that his 1980 cobalt
oxide cathode provided higher voltage by housing lithium ions at the molecular level.

### Research-motif powers

#### Lithium Ion

**Real hook.** Goodenough's Nobel [facts page](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2019/goodenough/facts/)
describes cobalt oxide with spaces that house lithium ions in a higher-voltage battery.

**Game fiction.** Cartoon lithium ions orbit a shield and bank charge. This is not a battery model.

**Caption.** "Charge accumulates. The gloves stay on."

#### Cobalt Oxide Cathode

**Real hook.** The Nobel [2019 press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2019/press-release/)
identifies Goodenough's work in the development of rechargeable lithium-ion batteries.

**Game fiction.** A cobalt-oxide crystal becomes a personal barrier with impossible toughness.

**Caption.** "High voltage behind a very solid shield."

#### FULLY CHARGED

**Real hook.** The Nobel [popular information](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2019/popular-information/)
explains that the prize recognizes the rechargeable lithium-ion battery used in portable electronics
and electric vehicles.

**Game fiction.** A round's damage becomes one theatrical electrical discharge; no real device
stores combat damage.

**Caption.** "Every hit taken comes back with interest."

## Donna Strickland

### Prize record

Donna Strickland received the 2018 Nobel Prize in Physics, shared with Gerard Mourou, "for their
method of generating high-intensity, ultra-short optical pulses." The [Nobel Prize
summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2018/summary/) records the prize and citation.

### Likeness record

The references below are downloaded only to `/private/tmp/nobel-combat-m4-physics/`; they are not
game assets and are not part of the repository.

- **1985:** The [Space for Life account](https://m.espacepourlavie.ca/blogue/en/lasers-changed-life-donna-strickland)
  publishes a University of Rochester doctoral-research photograph at an ultrafast laser setup.
  The scratch copy is `strickland_1985_rochester.jpg`; the page was accessed 2026-09-24.
- **2018:** The [University of Waterloo Nobel ceremony page](https://uwaterloo.ca/news/nobel-prize-award-ceremony)
  provides a later professional portrait. Its downloadable image endpoint was unavailable during
  collection, so this page remains a cited reference rather than a second scratch image. The page
  was accessed 2026-09-24.
- **2023:** The [Nobel Prize summit profile](https://www.nobelprize.org/events/nobel-prize-summit/2023/panellists/donna-strickland-2/)
  supplies a later Nobel portrait. The scratch copy is `strickland_2023_nobel.jpg`; the page was
  accessed 2026-09-24.

The selected game period is the 1985 doctoral research period, directly tied to the original CPA
work. The 2023 portrait records a separate mature-career period and confirms that glasses are a
usable identity cue.

### Visual review

An `image_evaluator` inspected the two downloaded images. The 1985 research photograph shows a
young Strickland with short dark hair, large dark rectangular glasses, lab knitwear, and optical
equipment. It is grainy and does not provide a clean full-body turnaround. The 2023 portrait shows
blond-gray shoulder-length hair with bangs, dark rectangular glasses, a dark blazer, and white
collar. No period source found here supports the proposed curly brown hair. Correct the roster
reference to short dark hair with large dark rectangular glasses and practical laboratory knitwear;
do not blend mature hair into the doctoral-period model.

### Research-motif powers

#### Chirp

**Real hook.** The Nobel [2018 press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2018/press-release/)
explains that CPA first stretches a laser pulse in time, reducing peak power.

**Game fiction.** A stretched pulse crawls across the arena as a broad projectile.

**Caption.** "A stretched pulse takes the scenic route."

#### Amplify

**Real hook.** The Nobel [scientific background](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/10/advanced-physicsprize2018.pdf)
states that the stretched pulse is amplified in laser material without damaging it.

**Game fiction.** The amplifier becomes a self-buff with no laboratory hardware or hazard.

**Caption.** "The pulse is louder. The laser is still fine."

#### Pulse Compression

**Real hook.** The Nobel [press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2018/press-release/)
states that compressing the pulse packs light into a shorter interval and dramatically increases
intensity.

**Game fiction.** The result is a delayed arena blast, not an operating laser.

**Caption.** "An ultrashort pulse with an extremely short fuse."

## John Bardeen

### Prize record

John Bardeen received the Nobel Prize in Physics twice: in 1956, shared with William B. Shockley
and Walter H. Brattain, "for their researches on semiconductors and their discovery of the
transistor effect"; and in 1972, shared with Leon N. Cooper and John R. Schrieffer, "for their
jointly developed theory of superconductivity, usually called the BCS-theory." See the Nobel
[1956 summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1956/summary/) and [1972
summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1972/summary/).

### Likeness record

The references below are downloaded only to `/private/tmp/nobel-combat-m4-physics/`; they are not
game assets and are not part of the repository.

- **1948:** The [Heraldo transistor history](https://www.heraldo.es/noticias/sociedad/2023/01/17/75-anos-de-la-invencion-del-transistor-el-origen-de-una-tecnologia-revolucionaria-1624886.html)
  reproduces the Bell Labs group photograph. The scratch copy is `bardeen_1948_bell_labs.jpg`; the
  page was accessed 2026-09-24.
- **1950s to 1960s:** The [University of Illinois Bardeen collection finding aid](https://archon.library.illinois.edu/archives/?id=690&p=collections%2Ffindingaid)
  describes career-period photographs, including 1955-58 material and a 1963 desk portrait. The
  [1963 item record](https://archon.library.illinois.edu/archives/?id=3553&p=digitallibrary%2Fdigitalcontent)
  gives its date and University provenance; the download endpoint was unavailable during
  collection. Both pages were accessed 2026-09-24.
- **1956:** The [Nobel Prize 1956 summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1956/summary/)
  carries a period portrait. The scratch copy is `bardeen_1956_nobel.jpg`; the page was accessed
  2026-09-24.
- **Mid-century portrait:** [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bardeen.jpg)
  hosts a clear head-and-shoulders portrait. The scratch copy is `bardeen_portrait_wikimedia.jpg`;
  the page was accessed 2026-09-24.

The selected game period is the transistor and early superconductivity period, around 1956. The
1948 Bell Labs group and 1956 portrait are visual corroboration; the University of Illinois archive
establishes a second career-period institutional source.

### Visual review

An `image_evaluator` inspected the three downloaded images. The 1948 group picture shows a young

Because it contains three researchers, confirm his exact position from source context before final
modeling. The 1956 Nobel portrait visibly supports a dark suit, white shirt, striped tie, and round
glasses. It does not establish a blue suit or unusually thick glasses. Correct the roster reference
to a dark or neutral suit with round glasses; do not claim a blue suit, exact fabric color, or a
precise physique.

### Research-motif powers

#### Transistor

**Real hook.** The Nobel [1956 summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1956/summary/)
credits Bardeen and colleagues with semiconductor research and discovery of the transistor effect.

**Game fiction.** A tiny input attack controls a comically oversized output strike.

**Caption.** "A tiny input controls a wildly large output."

#### Cooper Pair

**Real hook.** The Nobel [1972 press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1972/press-release/)
describes the BCS theory's explanation of superconductivity.

**Game fiction.** Two symbolic electrons travel as a paired projectile through an imaginary arena.

**Caption.** "Two electrons, one shared appointment."

#### BCS Superconductivity

**Real hook.** The Nobel [1972 summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1972/summary/)
names the jointly developed BCS theory as the prize-winning work.

**Game fiction.** The arena reaches impossible dramatic cold and generates a persistent-current
attack; it is not a physical superconductivity simulation.

**Caption.** "Resistance drops. The current keeps coming."

## Gerhard Herzberg

### Prize record

Gerhard Herzberg received the 1971 Nobel Prize in Chemistry "for his contributions to the knowledge
of electronic structure and geometry of molecules, particularly free radicals." His [Nobel Prize
biographical record](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1971/herzberg/biographical/)
identifies the award year and the official Nobel source.

### Likeness record

The references below are downloaded only to `/private/tmp/nobel-combat-m4-physics/`; they are not
game assets and are not part of the repository.

- **Circa 1950:** The [University of Saskatchewan gallery](https://research.usask.ca/herzberg/photos.php)
  presents the National Research Council photograph of Herzberg in a lab coat at a blackboard. The
  scratch copy is `herzberg_c1950_blackboard.jpg`; the page was accessed 2026-09-24.
- **1952:** [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Herzberg,_Gerhard_1952_London.jpg)
  hosts the London portrait. The scratch copy is `herzberg_1952_london.jpg`; the page was accessed
  2026-09-24.
- **1970:** The [National Research Council digital repository](https://nrc-digital-repository.canada.ca/eng/view/object/?id=5228956b-11b0-4582-8015-c621858bca9b)
  identifies its studio portrait as a 1970 image from the Gerhard Herzberg Fonds. The scratch copy
  is `herzberg_1970_nrc.jpg`; the page was accessed 2026-09-24.

The selected game period is the circa-1950 to 1952 spectroscopy career. The 1970 archive portrait
is a second, later career period that makes the appearance change explicit rather than silently
mixing it into the earlier model.

### Visual review

An `image_evaluator` inspected all three scratch photographs. The circa-1950 blackboard image is
the strongest anchor: slicked dark hair with a high or receding hairline, clean-shaven face, white
lab coat over vest and tie, and glasses held in one hand. It does not establish that he wore glasses
on his face, and the coat crop does not establish a slim build. The 1952 London portrait confirms
formal dark suit, glasses, and swept hair but is small. The 1970 portrait shows swept-back gray
hair, suit, wide striped tie, and a serious expression. Use the circa-1950 laboratory silhouette
and face cues; use neither the chalkboard diagrams nor the later gray hair as identity shorthand.

### Research-motif powers

#### Absorption Spectrum

**Real hook.** Herzberg's Nobel [biographical record](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1971/herzberg/biographical/)
records the prize for molecular electronic structure and geometry, the subject studied through
molecular spectroscopy.

**Game fiction.** A missing wavelength becomes a defensive counterattack; no real spectrum absorbs
an opponent's attack.

**Caption.** "The beam disappears. The return address is you."

#### Free Radical

**Real hook.** The Nobel [1971 prize summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1971/summary/)
gives the official citation for Herzberg's contributions to molecular electronic structure and
geometry, particularly free radicals.

**Game fiction.** A cartoon radical homes on an opponent. It is a character effect, not a chemical
prediction.

**Caption.** "One unpaired electron; zero chill."

#### Molecular Spectrum

**Real hook.** Herzberg's Nobel [lecture page](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1971/herzberg/lecture/)
is a primary Nobel record for the molecular-spectroscopy research represented by spectral lines.

**Game fiction.** Spectral lines fire as colorful arena projectiles; real spectroscopy is a method
of measurement, not a weapon.

**Caption.** "The dark is full of very specific wavelengths."
