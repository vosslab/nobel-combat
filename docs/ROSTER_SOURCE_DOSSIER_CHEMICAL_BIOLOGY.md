## Carolyn Bertozzi

### Prize record

- **Prize:** Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2022, one-third share.
- **Official citation:** "for the development of click chemistry and
  bioorthogonal chemistry." [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2022/bertozzi/facts/)

### Proposed fighter concept

The proposed roster names Bertozzi as a casual academic chemical biologist who
acts on selected targets in a living opponent without disrupting its ordinary
biology. It gives her the specials **Bioorthogonal Reaction**, **Click**, and
**Glycocalyx**.

### Scientific hooks and fictional specials

1. **Bioorthogonal Reaction** - Saxon and Bertozzi demonstrated a modified
   Staudinger reaction for selective cell-surface engineering in 2000
   ([primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.287.5460.2007)). **Fictional
   caption:** "Everything else is chemistry; _you_ are the only tagged atom."
   The arena-scale target selection is deliberate fiction.
2. **Click** - Bertozzi's group reported copper-free click chemistry for
   dynamic in-vivo imaging, allowing reaction design compatible with living
   systems ([primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0707090104)).
   **Fictional caption:** "Two harmless pieces. One extremely committed
   connection." The instantaneous restraint is a comic exaggeration.
3. **Glycocalyx** - the same in-vivo imaging work used chemical reporters to
   visualize glycans, the sugar-rich material at cell surfaces
   ([primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0707090104)). **Fictional
   caption:** "Surface sugars: now with arena-sized fluorescent upholstery."
   A giant visible sugar coat is fiction, not a biological claim.

## K. Barry Sharpless

### Prize record

- **Prize:** Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2001, one-half share, "for his work on
  chirally catalysed oxidation reactions." [Nobel Prize facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2001/sharpless/facts/)
- **Second prize:** Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2022, one-third share, "for the
  development of click chemistry and bioorthogonal chemistry." [Nobel Prize
  facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2022/sharpless/facts/)

### Proposed fighter concept

The proposed roster uses a casual laboratory chemist with receding gray hair
and glasses, whose fast, reliable reactions culminate in **Sharpless
Epoxidation**, **Click**, and **Second Nobel**.

### Scientific hooks and fictional specials

1. **Sharpless Epoxidation** - Katsuki and Sharpless reported a practical
   asymmetric epoxidation method in 1980 ([primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1021/ja00538a077)).
   **Fictional caption:** "One oxygen atom, placed with unreasonable
   confidence." A literal ring around an opponent is a visual joke.
2. **Click** - Kolb, Finn, and Sharpless defined click chemistry as a family
   of reliable, selective reactions for rapid synthesis
   ([primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/1521-3773%2820010601%2940:11%3C2004::AID-ANIE2004%3E3.0.CO;2-5)).
   **Fictional caption:** "The projectiles were merely introductions; the
   bond did the paperwork." The flying projectiles are fiction.
3. **Second Nobel** - the two official Nobel records document separate
   Chemistry prizes in 2001 and 2022 for asymmetric oxidation and click
   chemistry respectively ([2001 facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2001/sharpless/facts/),
   [2022 facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2022/sharpless/facts/)).
   **Fictional caption:** "The combo ended in 2001. Then the meter remembered
   2022." A second meter and combat finisher are entirely fictional.
